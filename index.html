About Me

I am Zhenyi Zhang (张镇驿), a PhD candidate in the Space Geodesy Group at ETH Zurich, supervised by Prof. Benedikt Soja.

My research builds on a strong background in space geodesy, atmospheric science, and machine learning. My doctoral work focuses on physics-informed machine learning for tropospheric modeling, integrating geodetic observations, meteorological data, and physical knowledge. I developed the first global AI-based models for asymmetric tropospheric mapping functions and direct slant wet delay prediction.

I also contribute to the Earth System Foundation Model (ESFM) project and the Swiss AI Initiative. My contribution to ESFM focuses on integrating satellite water vapor observations into the model. More broadly, I explore physics-informed frameworks that combine multi-source satellite observations and meteorological data for atmospheric modeling and prediction, with applications in weather forecasting.

Previously, I received my MSc in Geodesy and Surveying Engineering and BSc in Navigation Engineering from Wuhan University. My master's research, supervised by Prof. Yidong Lou and Prof. Weixing Zhang, focused on high-precision processing of ground-based and highly dynamic airborne GNSS observations, as well as the generation of GNSS-derived tropospheric products. This work supported applications in data assimilation, numerical weather prediction, and InSAR atmospheric correction.

Research Interests

  • AI for Science
    Physics-informed machine learning and foundation models for atmospheric and Earth-system science, with a focus on integrating satellite water vapor observations into ESFM.
  • GNSS data processing & meteorology
    Precise processing of ground-based and highly dynamic airborne GNSS observations, tropospheric estimation, and atmospheric water vapor retrieval, with applications in GNSS data assimilation for numerical weather prediction and InSAR atmospheric correction.
  • Physics-constrained atmospheric modeling
    Physics-constrained modeling of tropospheric delays, including physically consistent mapping functions and direct slant wet delay prediction.
  • Geodetic observations for climate research
    Applications of geodetic observations, particularly GNSS-derived tropospheric products, to climate monitoring and prediction, including ENSO prediction and changes in the global distribution and vertical structure of atmospheric water vapor.
